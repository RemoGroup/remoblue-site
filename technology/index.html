The architecture, explained

One business brain.
Many ways to work.

Your company knowledge informs RemoGM. Remo coordinates supported work across software, people, and compatible humanoids—with your direction and authority at the center.

Company knowledge & industry expertise

Your products, customers, procedures, service standards, and goals provide business context. Industry workflows help organize that context around the work you choose.

RemoGM & owner authority

Remo plans, coordinates, and tracks within the goals, permissions, and approvals you set. Updates and decisions return to the owner, with a way to pause work and resolve exceptions.

Software & people

Connected applications and your team are different ways to carry out supported work. Clear assignments and follow-up return progress, results, and requests for help to RemoGM.

Compatible humanoids & local controls

Business-task coordination passes supported priorities and constraints to a validated integration. The robot’s sensing, movement, and local controls govern physical execution. Compatibility and task validation are required; humanoid integration is in development.

Illustrative future workflow / Retail

One store. One task.
A complete follow-through.

A stock discrepancy becomes a clear assignment, a checked result, and a next step. Here’s the experience we’re working toward.

  1. 01

    Notice.

    Remo identifies a difference between the stock records and the information available from the store.

  2. 02

    Assign.

    A shelf-check task goes to a compatible robot with the validated ability and permission to perform it.

  3. 03

    Verify.

    The device returns its observation. Remo checks whether it resolves the original question.

  4. 04

    Follow through.

    Remo prepares the inventory follow-up or brings an unresolved discrepancy to the designated person.

Illustrative scenario, not a completed demonstration. Physical capabilities depend on the robot, environment, integration, and task validation.

A closer look

The questions
behind the vision.

What does “training your humanoid” mean today?

Start by teaching Remo your company’s information, approved procedures, standards, and priorities. That knowledge shapes the digital responsibilities supported by your setup. Teaching physical skills to a robot is a separate engineering and validation process.

How would RemoGM become part of a humanoid?

Through a software integration connecting business context and supported tasks to the robot’s capabilities. The design can use onboard computing, connected services, or a combination, depending on the manufacturer and validated deployment. RemoGM’s business intelligence and the robot’s movement controls have distinct responsibilities.

Will it work with any humanoid?

The direction is to support multiple compatible platforms. Each robot and task still needs its own integration and validation. We are not announcing universal compatibility or a production-ready hardware integration on this page.

What can I use now?

RemoGM is being introduced through configured business pilots. We’ll demonstrate the digital responsibilities available for your setup and explain what needs your approval. Humanoid connections and physical tasks are in development. Discuss your business setup.

Can manufacturers discuss the technical details?

Yes. This page provides a public architecture overview. Detailed interfaces, implementation methods, and manufacturer-specific work are discussed privately with qualified engineering partners. Contact us about an integration.
